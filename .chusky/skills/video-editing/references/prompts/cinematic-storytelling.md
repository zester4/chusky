# Cinematic storytelling

Use a beginning state, a motivated action, a visual transition, and a clear ending state. Keep continuity descriptors identical across shots.

1. Create a 16:9, 12-second opening scene: at dawn, an original courier waits beneath a green awning in a fictional coastal town, hears a distant bell, and looks toward an empty road. Begin wide and still, then make a slow push to the face; wind moves only the paper notice, muted sea ambience, restrained film grain.
2. Create a 16:9, 15-second intimate scene in a fictional train carriage. An adult woman reads an unbranded postcard, folds it once, and looks through the window as lights pass across her face. Use a gentle lateral track, maintain the same blue coat and short hair, window reflections natural, low carriage hum, no readable postcard text.
3. Create a 2.39:1, 10-second suspense beat in an empty archive. An original investigator opens one drawer, finds a small brass key, and hears a door close behind them. Start overhead, descend to a close-up of the key, end on a slow turn toward darkness; cool fluorescent light, controlled breathing, no violence.
4. Create a 16:9, 15-second magical-realism scene in a quiet apartment. A paper crane on a table lifts in a beam of afternoon light, circles one lamp, and settles back down as an adult watches. Use a slow orbit, physically coherent shadow, warm dust motes, soft room tone, no familiar fantasy character or copied visual style.
5. Create a 16:9, 12-second reunion scene at a rainy bus stop. Two original adult siblings recognize each other, cross one step, and share a restrained embrace. Start in a wide shot through wet glass, rack focus between them, end close on hands; preserve coats and faces, rain and traffic ambience, natural emotion.
6. Create a 9:16, 8-second silent micro-story: a childless adult gardener finds the first green shoot in a cracked rooftop planter, touches the soil, and smiles. Macro opening, tilt to face, sunrise backlight, gentle city sound, no dialogue, no child imagery, consistent hand anatomy.
7. Create a 16:9, 15-second historical-fiction-inspired scene with entirely fictional identities and no real insignia. An adult mechanic repairs a bicycle in a sunlit workshop, hears an approaching storm, and rolls the bicycle indoors. Use warm-to-cool lighting transition, one continuous dolly, authentic tools and motion, no period trademark or copied film look.
8. Create a 16:9, 10-second quiet comedy beat. An adult chef carefully carries a towering fictional cake through a narrow kitchen; a swinging door opens, the chef freezes, then safely sets it down. Use a medium tracking shot and one reaction close-up, believable weight, warm practicals, subtle utensil clatter, no slapstick damage.
9. Create a 2.39:1, 15-second discovery shot in a fictional desert observatory. An astronomer steps outside, removes protective glasses, and watches a faint green aurora appear above the dome. Crane from boots to sky, preserve silhouette and wardrobe, restrained orchestral swell, no recognizable spacecraft or franchise imagery.
10. Create a 16:9, 12-second closing scene in a quiet library at night. An original adult closes a notebook, switches off one lamp, and leaves a narrow pool of light on an empty chair. Slow dolly backward, consistent warm shadows, page and room tone, a clear final hold, no readable text.
