# Buyer tactics and calm counters

Recognize the move; respond without heat.

| Tactic | What it sounds like | Response |
|--------|---------------------|----------|
| **Lowball** | “We only have $X.” | Treat as aspiration, not fact. Ask constraints; offer scoped package at $X or full value at target. |
| **Split the difference** | “Meet me in the middle.” | Middle of a bad anchor is still bad. Re-center on value package. |
| **Competitor quote** | “They’re 20% cheaper.” | Compare scope, risk, support, total cost — not sticker only. Ask for comparable line items. |
| **Budget was cut** | Late-stage squeeze | Acknowledge; re-phase or reduce scope; avoid silent margin bleed. |
| **Good cop / bad cop** | Champion vs procurement | Give procurement a **documentable** win (term, ramp, training) that isn’t pure list collapse. |
| **Nibbling** | “And throw in training?” after yes | “Happy to add that if we adjust term/fee — shall I revise?” |
| **Deadline pressure** | “Sign today or lose the rate” (from them or false from us) | Real deadlines only. Prefer decision dates both sides own. |
| **Negotiauction** | Playing vendors against each other | Don’t race to the bottom; clarify evaluation criteria; strengthen BATNA. |
| **Take it or leave it** | Ultimatum | Stay calm; restate floor; offer one alternative structure; be willing to walk. |

## Procurement

Procurement is often scored on **savings**. Help them show a win:

- Multi-year efficiency, prepaid savings, removed unused modules
- Implementation credits tied to milestones
- Clear “was / now” commercial summary for their internal memo

Do not insult procurement. Partner with the champion to package the win.

## When the owner is the **buyer**

Same skill, inverted:

- Prepare BATNA (other vendors, delay, in-house)
- Ask for structure, not only price
- Trade volume, term, referenceability for better rates
- Stay polite; still use if/then
