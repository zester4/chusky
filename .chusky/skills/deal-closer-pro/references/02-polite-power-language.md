# Polite power language

Tone: **peer, not petitioner; partner, not predator.**

## Core moves

| Move | Example |
|------|---------|
| Acknowledge | “I hear that budget is tight this quarter.” |
| Reframe to interest | “Help me understand what the 15% needs to unlock internally.” |
| Value anchor | “Teams like yours typically see X within Y months when we keep full onboarding.” |
| Conditional trade | “If we move to a 24-month term, I can meet you on the implementation fee.” |
| Soft no + alternative | “I can’t do 25% off list and still deliver the support you need. Here’s a structure that works.” |
| Calibrated question | “What would make this a clear yes on your side?” / “How should we phase this so finance is comfortable?” |
| Label (tactical empathy) | “It seems like procurement needs a visible win on paper.” |

## Calibrated questions (prefer how / what)

- “What problem must this solve in the first 90 days?”
- “How are you comparing options beyond unit price?”
- “What happens if this slips a quarter?”
- “Which terms matter most after price — term length, payment, support, liability?”

Avoid interrogation energy. One or two sharp questions beat a barrage.

## What polite is not

- Apologizing for price (“Sorry it’s so expensive…”)
- Undermining your offer (“We might be flexible…” before any ask)
- Fake urgency or false scarcity
- Talking over them or correcting them publicly

## Silence

After a clear offer or trade, **pause**. Let them respond. Filling silence with
a spontaneous discount is how margin dies.

## Writing (email / chat)

- Short paragraphs, one ask, clear next step
- Summarize *their* constraints accurately before your counter
- No ALL CAPS pressure, no guilt
- End with a concrete path: call, revised proposal, or decision date
