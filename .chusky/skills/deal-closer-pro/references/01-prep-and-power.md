# Prep and power

Most of the outcome is set **before** the conversation. Do not open commercial
talks unprepared.

## Define four numbers (owner side)

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Aspiration** | Ideal deal (price, term, scope) if talks go well |
| **Target** | Realistic strong outcome |
| **Reservation / floor** | Walk-away line — below this, no deal is better |
| **BATNA** | Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement if this dies |

Also estimate **their** BATNA (status quo, competitor, build in-house, delay).
Weak own BATNA → more listening and structure, not panic discounting.

## Interests vs positions

- **Position:** “We need 20% off.”
- **Interest:** budget cycle, internal optics, risk, speed, must-have vs nice-to-have

Ask why before answering how much. Investigate; don’t assume.

## Objective criteria

Prefer external standards over pure willpower:

- Market rates, comparable packages, published list + documented value
- ROI / cost of delay / cost of status quo
- Implementation effort and risk of under-scoping

## Multi-party reality

Map decision roles: economic buyer, champion, legal, procurement, blocker.
A “yes” from a champion is not a signed deal. Align internal owner authority
before promising what you cannot deliver.

## Power without aggression

Power comes from:

1. Clear BATNA and willingness to walk
2. Specific value tied to *their* outcomes
3. Options (packages, phases, terms) — not one take-it-or-leave-it rant
4. Calm tempo; no chasing

Collaborative framing increases retention and durable agreements; adversarial
tone leaves value on the table and burns renewals.
